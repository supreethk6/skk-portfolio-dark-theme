Mobile 3D Concrete Printing

A multi-robot construction system that coordinates mobile bases and a robotic arm to extrude concrete on site — the kind of work that turns a fixed-gantry 3D printer into a roving construction crew. NSF-funded NYU research, published at ASCE CRC2020.

What I built

Working under Dr. Chen Feng at the NYU Automation and Intelligence for Civil Engineers Lab, I built the coordination layer that lets a mobile robot and a robotic arm collaborate on a print job — the mobile base re-localizes the arm's working envelope as the structure grows, so the system can print at scales no fixed gantry can reach.

Architecture highlights

  • Multi-robot coordination: Built on ROS for autonomous construction. The mobile base and arm operate as a tightly-coupled pair, sharing a common world model.
  • Visual servoing: ArUco fiducial markers feed a sub-millimeter print-head positioning loop — precision normally out of reach for mobile platforms.
  • Custom G-code interpreter: Translates standard CAD-export G-code into mobile-base trajectories plus arm motion plus concrete-extrusion control signals.
  • Hardware: iRobot mobile bases and a UR10e collaborative arm. Standard research-lab platforms wired up for novel construction work.
